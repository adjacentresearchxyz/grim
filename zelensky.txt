Background on the Russia-Ukraine War
The Russia-Ukraine war escalated into a full-scale conflict on February 24, 2022, when Russia, under President Vladimir Putin, launched a large-scale invasion of Ukraine, aiming to seize territory, topple the government, and prevent Ukraine’s integration with the West, particularly NATO. This followed years of tension, including Russia’s annexation of Crimea in 2014 and support for separatists in eastern Ukraine’s Donbas region. The war has caused immense destruction, displaced millions, and drawn significant international involvement, with the U.S. and European nations providing Ukraine with billions in military and economic aid to counter Russian aggression.
By early 2025, the conflict remains unresolved, with Russia occupying roughly 20% of Ukrainian territory, including parts of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, and Kherson oblasts, plus Crimea. Ukraine has mounted a resilient defense, including a notable incursion into Russia’s Kursk region in August 2024, aimed at gaining leverage in potential negotiations. However, the front lines have seen grueling attrition, with Russia making slow gains in the east (e.g., near Pokrovsk) while Ukraine struggles with manpower and resource shortages.
Zelenskyy’s Presidency
Volodymyr Zelenskyy, a former comedian, was elected president in May 2019 with 73% of the vote, promising to fight corruption and resolve the Donbas conflict diplomatically. His presidency took a dramatic turn with Russia’s 2022 invasion, transforming him into a wartime leader. His term officially ended in May 2024, but martial law, enacted after the invasion, prohibits elections during wartime, extending his tenure indefinitely. This has sparked debate about his legitimacy, with critics like Putin and U.S. President Donald Trump calling him a “dictator,” though Zelenskyy maintains he serves at the will of Ukrainians, who still show significant support (polls in late 2024 suggest trust around 50-57%).
